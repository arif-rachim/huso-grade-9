## Rules for writing material (easy to understand and engaging)
1. **Start from their world, then the concept.** Every material file opens with a short *hook*
   (2–3 sentences) from one of the three interests: kitchen, Valorant, or the pool. Then explain
   the concept, then come back to the hook in an example or a question.
2. **Picture first, text second.** Every concept that can be drawn gets a coloured SVG diagram
   (subject colour). Maximum 6 short points per section; bold the keyword.
3. **Simple, friendly English**, like an older student explaining: short sentences, "you", a
   14-year-old's vocabulary, every technical term explained the first time it appears.
4. **Analogies must be correct.** An analogy is only a door in: when it is not 100 % accurate,
   add one sentence starting **"The difference:"** so it does not cause a misconception.
5. **Rotate the three themes** across a subject (not all Valorant). Use the theme that is most
   *natural* for the concept (see the idea bank below); never force it.
6. **Valorant**: focus on strategy, economy (credits), maps, rounds, agents and abilities, timing,
   footsteps, probability, not violence. Agent/map names may be mentioned (game facts); no
   official art or logos (copyright): draw simple shapes yourself. **Never present real game
   numbers as facts** (they change): write "suppose" and use simple made-up numbers.
7. End every material file with **"Redraw from memory"** and **"Check your understanding"**
   (see the Materi issues and `docs/MATERI.md`).

## Rules for writing questions (check-your-understanding, examples, question bank)
1. **Exam questions stay HUSO style** (English, A–E, one correct answer) so the student gets used
   to the real exam.
2. **About 40 %** of questions get a **theme context** (kitchen / Valorant / pool) as a word
   problem; the rest are pure exam style. The context must never change the maths/science.
3. **HUSO-style worked examples** in the material may be more "story-like" (e.g. "Jett dashes…",
   "A swimmer pushes off the wall…", "A recipe needs…") with illustrated solution steps.
4. Realistic numbers (swimmer speed ≈ 2 m/s, pool 25/50 m, cooking oil ≈ 180 °C, boiling water
   100 °C); simple made-up numbers for Valorant.
5. All questions are original and **solved again independently** before commit (`docs/CONTENT.md`).
