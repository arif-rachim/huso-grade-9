# 🟨 Chemistry: visual study material for HUSO Grade 9–10

Question level: the hardest questions look like the **USNCO National Part I**; most are much easier. The exam gives you a **periodic table and the constants** (h, F, c, R), but you must **memorize the formulas and common reactions**. Organic chemistry is at most about **15–20 %** of the points. The 2024 paper had **35 questions in 36 minutes**, so speed on the easy ones matters.

## How the subtopics connect
- **Moles** (Reactions & Stoichiometry) are the language of everything after it: solutions, titration, gases, equilibrium and thermochemistry all start with "convert to moles".
- **Acids & Bases → Titration → Solubility → Equilibrium** are one chain: pH and Ka, then curves and indicators, then Ksp, and finally K and ΔG.
- **Periodic trends and bonding → Functional Groups and Organic Reactions:** electronegativity decides which atom is δ⁺ (attacked) and which is δ⁻ (the attacker).
- **Qualitative Analysis** and **Lab Skills** turn all of it into things you can *do* at a lab bench.

## How to use one file (about 25 minutes per subtopic)
1. **Read** the material (about 12 minutes): start with the opening story, look at the pictures, read the core ideas, study the examples.
2. **Close it**, take a sheet of paper, and **redraw from memory** (5 minutes) using the checklist in the ✏️ section. In chemistry the **energy diagram, the titration curve, the structures with curved arrows** and the **periodic table arrows** are the most important drawings. Use colored pencils.
3. **Check your understanding** (5 minutes): 5 questions without opening the material. **You are READY if you get at least 4 out of 5.**
4. **Log what you got wrong** in your Error Log (3 minutes): the topic, why you got it wrong, and a sketch of the correct way. Always write the **units**.
5. Watch the video in the 🎥 section if you are still stuck.

> Subtopics that are not READY go on the **red list** and are repeated on the remedial days (9–13 Nov).

## Chemistry habits that win points
- **Moles first:** convert every amount to moles, use the **balanced equation** for the ratio, then convert back.
- **Units and signs:** kelvin in gas laws and ΔG, joules with R = 8.314, and check that ΔH, K and pH tell the same story.
- **Draw it:** a titration curve, an energy diagram or a Lewis structure on scrap paper often shows the answer.
- **Quick ones first:** colors, trends, definitions and names take seconds. Skip anything that takes more than two minutes and come back.
- **Eliminate:** in a five-choice question, two answers are often impossible (wrong units, wrong charge, wrong direction).
